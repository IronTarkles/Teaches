    Abomination

    Overview

    Set 20 years after the events of Frankenstein, the creature is still out in the world, but doesn't want to continue on alone. It has gathered some of the greatest minds to try and create it a companion, so it will not forever walk this earth alone.

    This is a grisly worker placement game where we'll "ethically source" organic materials to create limbs and assemble our creature and work to bring it to life piece by piece.

    We'll play over a set number of rounds, or until someone has fully assembled their creation and brought it to life!

    For clarity, we're playing with what started off as a community twist on the ruleset called the Igor variant. It was so well received that it is now officially recognized by the designers and featured on the game's website

    Gameplay overview

    Each round is broken up into a few different phases (all shown on one of your player aides)

    • Events
      • Where things will happen to one or all of us
    • City phase
      • Where we'll take turns putting workers out onto the main board or our playerboards
    • Lab phase
      • Simulatenous phase where we'll build parts, try and bring them to life, and preserve materials
    • Reset phase
      • Cleanup and prepare for the next round

    Event phase

    The first player will draw an event from the deck

    • They're double sided, so make sure you sneak one out of the pile with the scary guy on it (that's the creature)
    • Depending what round it is, we'll resolve the appropriate side of the card
    • There are a few different kids of events that can happen
      • Generic event: These will impact action spaces on the board for the round, can be negative or positive
      • Encounter: Thematically these are run ins with the creature or other characters
        • Cards that trigger "now" are resolved immediately, and target a specific player based on listed criteria
          • If there are multiple players that meet the requirements, the first player chooses who gets targeted
          • If a card directs you to an "entry", this is a bit of story in the rulebook
        • Cards that list a location for a trigger are traps where the creature will lie in wait for someone
          • The first player flips the card facedown so no one can see it
          • The target line of the card will list when it is triggered: for instance, when a player visits that location
          • The person who is holding the card can never set off the trigger themselves
    • Lastly, trigger effects from an icon on the card
      • A red guillotine means that there is a public execution. Flip up cadaver cards from the public square deck into the public square
      • A lightning storm icon means that we're able to flip any uncharged leyden jars on our player board to their charged side

    VARIANT RULES - There are some toekns on the round track that are round we'll skip (for instance round 2) - When we reach these tokens, we'll advance the round token past them - In the new round, an execution WILL happen in the event phase, even if the icon isn't present on the event

    City phase

    This is where the meat of the game happens. Starting with the first player and going clockwise, we'll take turns placing a single worker into an action space, then the next player will go.

    • Once someone places a worker into a space, that space is blocked until the end of the round
    • We've all got 2 types of workers. A larger scientist, and some smaller assistant meeples
    • (Use academy on the right side of the board for example)
    • Some action spaces require you to use the scientist, where others you can use either or
    • Some action spaces will give you a different reward based on which meeple yhou used to visit that space.

    The benefit a player gains from going to a space is printed above it, with the cost(if any) being printed below

    Bribe and bump space

    This action space allows players to visit locations that are currently blocked by meeples

    • The player choosing this action places their meeple in an already occupied space (following normal worker requirments and space costs)
    • The player places the bumped out meeple into an available bribe and bump space
    • The player taking the bribe and bump action then pays the indicated coins to the bumped player
    • A plyer can bump themselves for free (since you can't pay yourself)
    • Once all of these spaces are full, the action can no longer be taken during the current round

    First player space

    This space works a little bit different

    • Can't bump someone that has gone here
    • They get the first player marker for the next round
    • After players finish placing out all meeples, this player may move this meeple to a different action spot

    Playerboard

    Before talking about the rest of the action spaces, we'll talk about the player board to get a little context

    Dials

    • Each dial has a corresponding icon that you may see come up in the game
    • When you see the matching icon, go up on that dial
    • The different dials have different bonuses and maluses (yes that is the word for a negative bonus)
    • Ever dial has end game victory points for your final position
    • Humanity
      • Balance between the good and bad you're doing
      • Rewards reputation gain or loss, as well as negative end game points
      • The bottom space is locked. If you ever mess up so bad that you're at -10 humanity, you will never be able to gain humanity again, and will be losing 20 points at the end of the game
    • Repuation
      • Your standing as a scientist in the community
      • Rewards worker upgrades. 7 unlocks a new assistant, other spaces let you replace an assistant with a scientist
    • Expertise
      • How capable you are at building the creature
      • Rewards upgrades to dice you'll use to animate your creature
    • Losing any of these bonuses means that you lose them!
      • Going down on humanity could cause you to lose reputation, which in turn could downgrade a worker

    Materials

    Cubes represent the different materials

    • Brown cubes are muscles
    • Purplish cubes are organs
    • Red cubes are blood
    • Yellow cubes are animal parts
    • White cubes are bones

    When you gain materials, they go in one of the zones on your playerboard

    • Bones always go to the bone zone
    • Other materails go into one of the 4 numbered zones based on how fresh they are
    • These spaces represnt the decay of your organic materials. Stage 1 is the freshest, stage 4 is the oldest

    You'll mainly gain materials by harvesting cadaver cards

    • Cadaver cards will give you 2 options
      • Gain expertise (the brain dial)
      • Gain materials (cubes to playerboard)
    • Cadaver cards will show how fresh the materials retreived from them are, and the quantity of which types of materials you'll earn from harvesting them
    • Any time you resolve a cadaver card, it is returned to the bottom of the stack it came from

    You can never hold more than 12 bones, and can never have more than 15 total parts of a single freshness at any time

    Action spaces

    Lets talk about all the action spaces where you can get materials. We'll go in order of least fresh to most fresh

    Cards usually have a choice on them, gain expertise or gain materials

    Some cards have a humanity loss as a cost listed in the top left

    Whenever you resolve a card, it will go back to the bottom of the deck it came from, there are no discard piles

    Material gathering spaces

    • Cemetery
      • Draw 3 cards, then resolve as many of them as you want
      • Only stage 3, 4, and straight up bones here with some cadavers losing you humanity
    • Morgue
      • Costs a coin to go here
      • Draw 2 cards, then resolve as many of them as you want
      • Some stage 2 and stage 3 cadavers here, with some losing you humanity
      • Some cadavers cost a humanity to take
    • Hospital (top action)
      • At the top of the round, cadavers equal to the number of players are put here with only the top one visible
      • The number of cadavers you get to take is dependent on your reputation
        • 1-6 rep gets you nothing
        • 7-14 rep gets you a card
        • 15 plus rep gets you a card, and you can optionally grab a second card by paying 1 coin
      • Cadavers here are stage 1 and 2, and will never cost you humanity to take
      • After resolving a card here put it on the bottom of the hospital deck, not back with the cadavers revealed in the space
        • This space isn't replenished until the next round
    • Public Square
      • Only populated in a round where an execution occurs as part of the event
      • Pay a coin and take a single cadaver
      • All stage 1 and give you a lot of materials
      • Cards from here also go back to their deck, not the board.
      • This space will only replenish whenever new executions take place
    • Slaughterhouse
      • Only directly provides animal parts
      • Gain 3 level 1 parts or 4 level 2 parts
    • Docks
      • You can outsource your material colleciton here
      • Pay the cost in the top left of the scoundrel you want to use
      • Resolve card, then put on bottom of deck. Immediately replenish from the top of the deck
    • Straight up Murder
      • Fresh materials at a steep price
      • Lose 3 humanity
      • Gain the attention of the police by grabbing a badge
      • Once you have 2 badges you can't murder again until you pay 3 to bribe away your badges

    Next we'll talk about actions that get you different bonuses

    Academy

    Go here to get research cards or gain repuation

    • Top space gains you a research card, which you select form the two here then replenish immediately
    • Put the card in front of you
    • Other actions here
      • Lecture to gain coins and rep
      • Donate to pay coins to get rep

    Saint Roch (Church)

    Go here to get humanity cards

    • Draw one of the 2 humanity cards, replenishing immediately
    • Put the card in front of you

    Cards

    • You may hold at most 3 of each type of card
    • You must discard a card to make room for a new one
    • All cards are one time use. After playing them return them to the bottom of the relevant deck
    • Some cards can be played whenever, other detail the circumstances in which they can be played
    • Play a card to gain the benefit on it. Research cards have a small bar at the bottom relating to mitigating dice rolls.
      • These are an alternate use of the card we'll talk about more soon
    • Generally the research cards further your goals, while humanity cards increase your humanity

    Hospital

    We skipped over the other actions here when talking about getting materials

    • Work lets you gain an amount of coins based on your expertise
    • Volunteer gets you a humanity and 2 reputation

    Market

    Space where you can buy and sell. Perform any transactions any number of times

    • Trade in bones or certain preserved materials for money
    • Pay money for leyden jars (used in reanimating your creature)
      • Put on your playerboard uncharged
      • Put onto your board charged if there was a lightning event this round
    • Pay money for ice to help preserve materials and prevent decay
      • Ice goes on your playerboard (on the half melted side VARIANT RULE, IT ONLY LASTS ONE ROUND)

    Playerboard actions

    • Gain expertise
    • Gain blood (probably from yourself)
    • Repair lets you take 3 damage tokens off your creature (more in a moment)
    • Charge leyden jars (Scientist will actually charge all 4 VARIANT RULE), you just flip 'em over

    Lab phase

    That's it for actions in the city phase. On to the lab phase, where we all act simultaneously

    Building monster parts

    • Player aides show costss for starting and completing parts
    • You have to start the part (build the gross muscley one) first before you can complete it (with skin and shit)
    • Building parts will show how much expertise is required to start/complete the part
      • Pay the required components. These can come from anywhere on your playerboard besides the preserved materials (the heart shaped box below the numbered areas)
      • Score points based on the oldest material you put into the body part
      • You can sub in animal parts as a wild for anything, but using at least one will be the indicated number of negative parts
        • Only restriction is stage 3 or 4 animal parts can't sub for blood (because blood can only be stage 1 or 2)
    • You'll then put the part onto your board or flip it over. No matter which, you'll gain 1 expertise
    • you can construct or complete any number of parts in a single lab phase if you can pay for multiple

    Throw the switch

    Attempt to animate your body parts

    • You can do this once per lab phase, must have at least one completed body part
    • Flip over between 1 and 3 leyden jars
    • Take a number of gray dice equal to twice the jars you spent
    • For every blue die symbol you've reached on the Expertise track, replace a gray die for a blue die
    • Roll the dice
      • For each lightning bolt, place a damage token on a body part
        • Each body part can hold no more than one damage token
        • If you would place a second, it will either downgrade or be discarded based on what state it is in
      • For each eyeball put an alive counter on a completed body part
        • Each body part can only hold one alive token. Lose any you can't place
        • You can't put an alive token on the head until all your other parts have an alive token on them
      • For each humanity loss symbol you have a choice (VARIANT RULES)
        • Do nothing and keep your humanity
        • Pay a humanity to place an alive token on a completed body part
    • You can spend any left over charged leyden jars to re-roll any number of dice. You can do this multiple times
    • You can now use any research cards you still have for their alternate ability to mitigate some bad die rolls. Return cards to their deck after use

    End of round

    • Preserve
      • Move any muscle or organ cubes to the preservation space from anywhere. You can sell these at the market later. Can't have more than 9 cubes here.
    • Decay materials
      • All cubes in numbered areas move one to the right
      • Blood moving into stae 3 is discarded
      • Anything moving off of phase 4 is discarded
      • Anyone with an ice block does not decay materials, but instead discards the ice blocks (all ice blocks are single use VARIANT RULES)
    • Reset board
      • Discard all face up cards from main board to the bottom of their decks
      • Refill all areas appropriately
    • All workers come back
    • Advance the round marker
    • Person with first player marker starts a new round

    Bonus objectives

    There are a number of objectives we're racing for at the bottom left of the board

    • Some are evaluated at the end of the game
    • Some are granted to the first player to acheive them
    • They're all worth 10 points

    End of game

    The game ends when we play the last round, or whenever someone successfully reanimates all of their body parts

    • Points from each objective earned
    • Points from bonuses reached on dials
    • If the game ended by the round marker, player with highest humanity gets 5 points
    • Points for alive body parts (we're going by VARIANT RULES, not printed on player aide)
      • 5 points for each alive part
      • 5 bonus points for head (so 10 total for head)
